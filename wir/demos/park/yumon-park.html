Yumon Park

Species built from permutations of capabilities, hunting, fighting back and hiding across four bands. Every Yumon carries its own copy of one compiled WIR brain. It starts as the written rule, learns from its own life while it sleeps, and passes what it learned on to its children through its mother. Children can gain or lose a capability, and groups that drift apart become new species.

Populations

Capabilities

Each capability is carried by a limb, and the limb's size gene sets how well it works. Claws carry both climbing and clawing, so one gene moves both, and a capability on a limb a Yumon already has is four times likelier to appear. Every limb costs upkeep, a limb that shrinks away loses its capabilities, and wings must lift the body: a lineage that grows too heavy for its wings stops flying. Counts are species alive or founding.

How a life works

Each tick a Yumon senses the nearest threat, food, prey and mate as distance buckets, whether an attacker struck it and its odds against it, feeds them to its brain with its hunger, fatigue and hurt, and samples one of ten options from a softmax over the brain's keys. The written rule hunts before it grazes when prey is near. When struck, an armed Yumon fights back if its odds are even or better, and a shell or spines can brace. Its reward is how much the tick reduced its need (hunger, fatigue and hurt, squared), plus a kill reward, a little for each blow landed, 1 for getting away, and a bonus for mating and for giving birth. A killer eats first, up to half the body.

When it falls asleep it takes one policy-gradient step on its own weights over what happened since the last sleep, held near the weights it was born with. A child gets a blend of both parents' genes, small changes to all of them and a bigger one to one, may gain or lose a capability, and gets a copy of its mother's brain. When a Yumon dies its brain leaves the cache. Walls you draw block movement, sight and mating calls.
